Reflection on Parameter Tuning

My methodology was to isolate one variable at a time. I first swept temperature (0.2, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0, 1.5) across all three use cases, then held temperature at 0.7 to compare top-k (10, 50, 100) with top-p (0.5, 0.9, 0.95), and finally compared beam search (3 and 5 beams) against sampling. From this I chose creative blog: temperature 0.9, top-p 0.95; technical summary: 0.3, top-p 0.9; customer support: 0.6, top-p 0.85, with top-k disabled everywhere.

The creativity-consistency trade-off drove each choice. Marketing intros need personality, so I accepted more randomness and the risk of drift. Technical content needs precision, so I accepted blander, more repetitive wording to reduce invented specifications. Support replies sit in between: warm enough to avoid sounding cold, controlled enough to stay on topic.

Temperature had the biggest effect on quality. Below roughly 0.2 text turns rigid and repetitive; above about 1.2 coherence breaks down. Top-k and top-p controlled which words were available: top-k uses a fixed cut-off that can feel too narrow at 10 or loose at 100, while top-p adapts to the model's confidence and sounded more natural. The two interact, so a high temperature paired with a moderate top-p (around 0.85) stays creative but focused.

The most surprising lesson was that beam search is deterministic and fluent but can be generic, while sampling changes on every run, so reproducibility has to be set deliberately with a seed.

In production I would not rely on one configuration. I would keep per-use-case presets, run A/B tests on many prompts rather than one, add human review or automated fact checks for technical content, log parameters with outputs, and revalidate whenever the model changes.
